Saving Water-Damaged Hardwood Floors in Placentia, CA

Home / Blog / Hardwood Floor Water Damage Repair in Placentia, CA

Hardwood floors are one of the most valuable features in any home, and they are also among the most vulnerable to water damage. Cupping, crowning, buckling, staining, and mold in the subfloor beneath are all results of water reaching hardwood. Homeowners across Placentia, CA who have wood floors benefit from knowing the right response to water and the difference between a floor worth restoring and one that needs replacing.

What Water Does to Wood Floors

The term for wood is hygroscopic: it gains or loses moisture depending on its surroundings. Wood fibers expand as they take on moisture, which is what happens when a floor gets too wet. Cupping comes first: the edges of each board lift above its center, visibly showing that moisture is out of balance. With continued exposure, a board can buckle, lifting completely off the subfloor, or crown, its middle higher than the edges because the upper face dries quicker than the underside. Mold is another risk of long exposure, since it grows unseen between the subfloor and the hardwood and may not be found until the floor comes up.

Will Your Wood Floors Survive Water Damage?

Whether water-damaged hardwood can be saved depends on several factors: how quickly professional drying begins, the volume of water involved, how long the wood was wet, the type and finish of the hardwood, and the condition of the subfloor. If the water was clean and a professional begins drying inside of 24 to 48 hours, the hardwood is salvageable in many cases. Success depends on drying the wood gradually and under control, which lets it give up moisture evenly instead of cracking and splitting.

How Professionals Dry Hardwood Floors

Drying hardwood is not done the way ordinary structural drying is, and it calls for its own techniques. To pull moisture evenly from the wood, mats or panels go directly on the surface and create a controlled drying environment. Floor drying systems can also be used beneath the subfloor from crawl spaces. Slow and even is the rule: rush it and the surface dries ahead of the core, leaving permanent splits, cracks, and cupping.

Signs the Floor Cannot Be Saved

Replacement is usually the answer if the subfloor is compromised or has mold on it, the boards have buckled or delaminated extensively, sewage, floodwater, or other contaminated water touched the wood, or the floor stayed wet longer than 48 to 72 hours.
