Kitchen Water Damage: What Placentia Homeowners Should Know

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. We help Placentia homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

What It Looks Like

  • Cabinet doors that stick or no longer line up with each other
  • Toe kicks that have swollen, darkened, or pulled away from the cabinet base
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • Soft or discolored vinyl flooring near the refrigerator or along the cabinet line
  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Begin to Swell

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Shut Off the Leak

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Measure the Wet Area

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Remove Only Damage

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

The Dishwasher Cavity

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

Toe Kick Space

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. It gathers water that sits for weeks, and the resulting mold stays hidden unless the cabinets are removed.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

Flooring Over Flooring

It is common for a kitchen floor to be laid right over the previous one. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.
