Wet Carpet Drying Help in Placentia

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. In Placentia, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

What It Looks Like

  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Damp Base of the Walls

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.

Water Held in the Pad

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. Long after the top feels dry, that pad is still passing moisture up into the carpet and down into the floor.

Stains From Furniture

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.

What We Do

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Find the Edges

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry Every Layer

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.
